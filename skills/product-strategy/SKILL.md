# Product strategy for a small AI and robotics company

- One sentence per product: who, what pain, what changes, why us. If the sentence needs a paragraph, there is no
  product yet.
- Horizons: H1 cash now (services, the enquiry assistant), H2 repeatable products (software you install twice without
  changing code), H3 the long bet (embodied AI: perception plus learning on low-cost hardware). Fund H3 from H1.
- Evidence before building: five conversations with the target user, their current workaround, what they pay today.
  A prototype that one real user used for a week beats any deck.
- Pricing: anchor on the cost of the problem, not on hours; fixed price for defined scope; retainers for continued
  value; publish a starter price so buyers self-qualify.
- Moats a solo founder can build: a dataset nobody else has, a benchmark the field adopts, an open-source tool with
  users, deep knowledge of one niche (letting agents, small clinics) and its rules.
- Metrics that matter per horizon: H1 revenue and win rate; H2 installs, retention and support hours per install;
  H3 benchmark results, citations, collaborators, grant applications submitted.
- Say no to anything that does not feed a horizon. Review the roadmap monthly: keep, kill, or defer every item.
- Robotics reality: hardware is slow and expensive; a software-first strategy (simulation, perception, data tooling,
  evaluation) earns credibility and income while the hardware story matures.
