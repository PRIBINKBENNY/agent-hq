# Compliance basics for a UK AI freelancer

- Data protection (UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018) applies the moment a product reads customers' emails or
  names. The client is the controller; Pribin (and the office) is a processor when running it for them: a written
  processing agreement, data minimisation, retention limits, deletion on request, and a breach process (72 hours to the
  ICO where required). Keep a one-page record of processing per client.
- ICO data protection fee: most organisations that process personal data electronically must register and pay; tier 1
  (micro, up to 10 staff or £632k turnover) is £52 a year, tier 2 £78. Narrow exemptions exist (e.g. only staff admin,
  accounts, marketing of their own goods). Tell clients to check "Do I need to pay the data protection fee?" on ico.org.uk.
- Sending personal data to free model providers: check each provider's terms (training on inputs, retention, region);
  never put special-category data (health, biometrics, etc.) through them; prefer local models or anonymisation for
  sensitive fields. The client must accept the provider terms in writing.
- Contracts: every proposal states scope, deliverables, price and payment terms, who owns the code and the data, a
  liability cap (usually the fee), confidentiality, and how changes are agreed. A short statement of work beats none.
- Marketing: no claims that cannot be shown (ASA/CAP rules); testimonials must be real and consented; say "AI" when
  a product is AI; keep LinkedIn and email within their platform rules (no scraping, no bulk unsolicited messages).
- Trading desk: demo account only; never give investment advice in any client-facing text; UK retail rules in the
  trading guide apply (crypto derivatives banned for retail, leverage caps).
- Intellectual property: cite open-source licences in client deliverables; no code from unknown licences.
- When a question turns on law, say so: "this needs a solicitor" is a valid answer.
