# Money basics for a UK sole trader (updated 2026-10-20)
- Register for Self Assessment once trading income passes £1,000 in a tax year (the trading allowance covers the first
  £1,000); the tax year runs 6 April to 5 April; the online return and payment are due 31 January after the year ends.
- Set aside about 25-30% of profit for income tax and National Insurance (Class 4 on profits above the lower limit;
  Class 2 is now credited for most). Keep money for tax in a separate pot; it is not income.
- Making Tax Digital for Income Tax: quarterly digital updates via software are required from 6 April 2026 for gross
  self-employment plus property income over £50,000 (2024/25 return), from April 2027 over £30,000, from April 2028
  over £20,000. Gross income, not profit, decides. Keep records digital from day one so the switch is painless.
- Invoices must show: a unique sequential number, the date, your trading name and address, the client's name and
  address, a description of the work, the amount, payment terms and due date, and how to pay. Not VAT-registered
  below the £90,000 threshold: do not show VAT.
- Keep every invoice, receipt and bank line for at least 5 years after the 31 January deadline. Record income on the
  invoice date (or when paid, under cash basis: the default for small sole traders), expenses when incurred.
- Allowable expenses: software and hosting, a share of home working costs, equipment, training that keeps skills current,
  travel for clients (not commuting). Personal and business money kept apart; a separate bank account helps.
- Chase late invoices at 7 and 14 days overdue; statutory interest can be charged on late commercial payments.
- This guide is bookkeeping practice, not tax advice: HMRC guidance (gov.uk) governs, and figures change each April.
Sources: GOV.UK "Check when to sign up for Making Tax Digital for Income Tax" (phases 2026/2027/2028), Lloyds Bank MTD
guide (2026), GOV.UK Self Assessment deadlines and trading allowance pages, GOV.UK invoicing rules. Verify yearly.
